STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE

How to make a memory video today

You can make a meaningful tribute with ordinary editing software. AI animation is not required. Use only media you own or have permission to use.

STEP 1

Choose the purpose

  1. Decide the occasion: wedding, birthday, anniversary, remembrance, or family gathering.
  2. Choose the tone: joyful, peaceful, celebratory, or reflective.
  3. Agree who will watch it and whether it will be private or shared.
STEP 2

Gather the memories

  1. Select a small set of clear photos and any videos you have permission to use.
  2. Collect dates, captions, favorite sayings, and stories from family.
  3. Use original recordings when available; do not treat invented AI speech as a real recording.
  4. Keep a backup and avoid uploading sensitive material to unfamiliar services.
STEP 3

Build the story

  1. Start with a title and the person's name.
  2. Arrange photos in a natural order: early life, meaningful moments, and the celebration or message.
  3. Add short captions, a personal note, and music you are allowed to use.
  4. Leave pauses so viewers can take in each moment.
STEP 4

Edit the video

  1. Open a video editor you trust and create a new project.
  2. Import your chosen photos and clips, then place them on the timeline.
  3. Set each photo's duration, add gentle transitions, and adjust cropping so faces remain visible.
  4. Add captions and music at a comfortable volume; keep spoken words clear.
  5. Preview the full video before exporting.
STEP 5

Optional AI photo animation

  1. Only if you choose: use a reputable photo-animation service that permits your intended use.
  2. Check the service's privacy, retention, deletion, and consent terms before uploading.
  3. Use a photo you are authorized to use; follow any required family or estate permissions.
  4. Request subtle movement first, review the result for errors, and discard anything unsettling or misleading.
  5. Label the result as AI-generated or AI-animated. Keep the original photo too.

Important: AI motion is generated, not a recovered recording of the person actually moving.

STEP 6

Export and test

  1. Export a high-quality MP4 if your editor offers it.
  2. Watch the exported file from beginning to end on the device you will use at the event.
  3. Check sound, captions, image orientation, and the final frame.
  4. Keep a second copy and test playback offline if the venue has unreliable internet.

FOR WEDDINGS AND CELEBRATIONS

How to prepare the moment

Plan with the host

  1. Ask the couple or event host how they want the tribute introduced.
  2. Choose whether the video plays during a dance, before a toast, or in a quiet private viewing.
  3. Let close family know what to expect; never surprise someone with a sensitive tribute without considering their wishes.

Set up the screen

  1. Confirm the venue's screen, projector, speakers, connections, and file format.
  2. Test brightness and sound in the actual room.
  3. Have a trusted person ready to start and stop playback.
  4. Keep a non-AI version available as a backup.

Make a dance tribute

  1. Choose a song meaningful to the person and family, with permission to play it.
  2. Prepare a short video or slideshow timed to a suitable section of the song.
  3. Invite people to join the dance only if they wish; participation should never be pressured.
  4. Offer a quiet alternative for guests who prefer not to watch.

Projection and life-size display

A projection can make a video feel larger, but ordinary projection cannot create a physically present person. Results depend on the projector, screen or projection surface, lighting, viewing angle, and production setup. Test with the venue before promising a life-size effect.

FUTURE EXPERIENCE · VR

How a Together Again VR experience could work

This is a future design plan, not a currently available Evermore feature. A practical first VR prototype would use a pre-built virtual room and a clearly identified digital character rather than claiming to recreate a real person perfectly.

VR STEP 1

Prepare the memory

Choose an authorized portrait, a short biography, and family-approved details. Decide which details should not be included.

VR STEP 2

Create the virtual setting

Build a calm room such as a ballroom, garden, or family living room. Provide seated and standing options, comfort settings, and an easy exit.

VR STEP 3

Add the digital representation

Use an appropriately licensed avatar or AI-generated character, with consent and clear labeling. Voice recreation would need separate permission and safeguards.

VR STEP 4

Design the interaction

Start with simple actions: greeting, listening to a selected song, viewing photos, or following a choreographed dance. Avoid presenting generated dialogue as proof of what the real person thought or said.

VR STEP 5

Test for comfort and safety

Test with volunteers, offer a non-VR screen version, include clear controls to pause or leave, and avoid flashing effects or forced interaction. Let users choose the length and intensity.

VR STEP 6

Close the experience gently

End with a chosen message, a favorite memory, or a return to the real room. Let the user decide whether to save the generated scene or delete it.

OUR PROMISE

The memory is real. The AI experience is created.

Evermore cannot bring someone back from death or make an absent person physically present. It can help families preserve true memories and may one day provide AI-generated ways to visualize or celebrate them. We will identify generated media honestly and never claim an animation, cloned voice, or digital character is the actual person returning.

Consent and dignity

Use living people's likenesses only with their permission. For someone who has died, establish appropriate authority and consider their known wishes and family concerns before creating a likeness or voice.

Privacy and control

Keep memories private by default. Check upload and deletion policies, restrict access to event files, and do not submit another person's intimate or sensitive information without authorization.

Respect the moment

AI results can be inaccurate or emotionally intense. Tell viewers what they will see, provide an opt-out, and never pressure anyone to participate.

Honest expectations

Video animation, projection, and VR depend on different equipment and services. Costs, technical quality, and availability vary. Evermore will clearly mark each feature as available, in testing, or planned.
